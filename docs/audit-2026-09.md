# Engine audit, September 2026: findings, fixes, and what changed

An audit of the CC-PPO engine (`ppo/src/`) and the run artefacts in `ppo/runs/`
against the claims on slides 8–15 of the then-local `ppo/1_presentation.tex`.
This is a historical September 2026 snapshot. The slide source, private plan,
local run logs, models, and some figure inputs are not included in this checkout;
recomputation of all historical measurements requires those artifacts.
The final section identifies the original private planning reference.

**Status at September 14:** V5 and walk-forward runs had been launched.
Their completed results are recorded in [experiment notes §9c](experiment-notes.md#9c-v5-walkforward).
Shared-exam selection was implemented later; the final BTC node enables it.
See [the current design](../context.md#current-implementation-final-btc-configuration)
for the later learning-progress configuration. Findings and tables below retain
their original experiment scope.

## 1. What was found

### F1. The V4 ledgers were destroyed after the slides were made
`ppo/run_v4.sh` was relaunched on 2026-09-09 09:47 (`ppo/runs/v4.log`) and every
job was killed ~56 minutes later (rc=137). The engine opened every ledger with
mode `"w"` on start-up, so 16 of the 18 V4 run directories now hold header-only
`generations.csv`, `matches.csv`, `adversary_params.csv`, `trader_params.csv`,
`pbt_events.csv` and `trader_external.csv`. Only `population_seed47` and
`single_seed47` survived intact. The 18 `best_trader.zip` models (dated Sep 7)
and the three `*_eval.json` files survived, so the headline table reproduces
exactly (re-evaluated: coevolution −0.654, population −1.257, single −1.321),
but slides 10–13 (curriculum, Red Queen, drift, cost) rest on the PNGs and
`ppo/figures/data.json` with no underlying data. What survives is bundled in
a local archive (not in git).

**Fix.** `EngineConfig.overwrite` (default False): the engine refuses to start
in an `out_dir` whose `generations.csv` already has rows. `run_v5.sh` and
`run_walkforward.sh` check the same thing before launching anything.

### F2. The trader hall of fame was not a snapshot, and it overwrote trader 0
`engine.py` archived `best_trader.model.get_parameters()` directly. In
stable-baselines3 that dict aliases the live tensors, so the "snapshot" kept
tracking the live trader (including after PBT overwrote it). The adversary
scoring loop then borrowed trader 0's model to load archive entries into, and
its "restore" (`set_parameters(live_params)`) was a no-op because
`live_params` was the same aliased dict. Verified with a 10-line SB3 test:
payload shares storage (True), trader-0 weights restored afterwards (False),
trader 0 equals the archive entry afterwards (True).

Consequences, all in the coevolution arm only (the other arms have no
adversary and never enter that loop):
- From generation 1, trader 0 ended every generation as a clone (weights and
  optimizer state) of some other live trader. This is the "one literally
  identical trader pair from generation 5" reported on slide 13; the
  population arm shows 0 identical pairs in every generation.
- The adversary-side anti-cycling archive was inert: every "archived trader"
  was a live trader.
- Trader 0's logged fitness and hyperparameters refer to weights that no
  longer existed when they were logged.

**Fix.** `hall_of_fame.snapshot_parameters` deep-copies to CPU; the engine
loads archive entries into a dedicated shell network (`_hof_shell`) that is
never trained. `tests/smoke_test.py` asserts that no archive tensor aliases a
live trader and that scoring against the archive leaves trader 0 alone.

### F3. The hypothesis itself was never tested
`evaluate.py` bootstraps model-vs-buy-and-hold and model-vs-MA. The claim is
coevolution vs single-agent PPO and vs population-only. `compare_arms.py`
(new) reports P(A ≤ B) for every arm pair and baseline under three paired
bootstraps. On the frozen V4 models, 9 test segments, 6 seeds:

| P(coevolution ≤ X) | segments (n=9) | seeds (n=6) | hierarchical |
|---|---|---|---|
| single | 0.123 | 0.000 | 0.166 |
| population | 0.220 | 0.049 | 0.255 |
| buy & hold | 0.213 | 0.002 | 0.233 |
| MA crossover | 0.596 | 0.761 | 0.580 |

Coevolution beats single and population on 5 of 6 seeds. The seed-level test
says the ordering is reproducible across training runs on this window; the
segment-level test says it is not shown to generalise to new windows. The
hierarchical number is the one to quote.

### F4. Nine test segments cannot reach significance, and two more segments reverse the ordering
Per-segment paired differences on V4: coevolution − single has mean 0.67 and
sd 1.80, so ~30 segments are needed for |t| = 2; vs buy-and-hold ~63.

Binance had published July and August 2026 by the time of the audit. Neither
month had been downloaded or touched by any stage, and the models were frozen
on Sep 7, so they are legitimately held out. `build_features.py` (new,
reproduces the notebook pipeline bit-for-bit on all 77,457 shared rows)
builds `data/processed/test_ext.parquet`: Oct 2025 → Aug 2026, 11 segments.
On it, the V4 models score:

| arm | 9 segments (Oct 25–Jun 26) | 11 segments (Oct 25–Aug 26) |
|---|---|---|
| coevolution | −0.654 | −0.396 |
| population | −1.257 | −0.175 |
| single | −1.321 | −0.395 |
| buy & hold | −1.295 | −0.191 |
| MA crossover | −0.466 | −0.184 |

The two added windows are rallies (buy-and-hold Sharpe +2.3 and +7.3), where
coevolution's low-exposure policy scores +0.5 and +1.1 against population's
+2.4 and +7.0. **On the extended window coevolution's mean advantage
disappears** (P(coevolution ≤ population) = 0.59 hierarchical). Per regime
the picture is unchanged: coevolution wins the three bear segments by a wide
margin (−1.67 vs −4.80 population, −3.44 single) and loses every rally. The
honest statement is therefore the slide-9 one, with the magnitude now
measured: coevolution buys bear-regime robustness and pays for it in rallies,
and whether that nets out positive depends on the regime mix of the window.
Only the walk-forward (48 segments across bear, bull and sideways folds) can
say which.

### F5. Root cause of arms-race disengagement: a fitness ceiling at zero
The adversary's score is −(trader Sharpe on the segments it served), and the
trader is scored on those segments *after* training on them (in-sample). In a
long-only environment with a trade penalty, the trader's safe answer to any
hard window is to hold cash, which scores Sharpe exactly 0. So once the
trader has learned "cash when in doubt", every adversary's best attainable
score is ≈ 0, every genome attains it, and the landscape has no gradient.

The surviving seed-42 trace (`runs/param_trace`, V3 config) shows exactly
that: top-4 adversary raw fitness 1.90 → 4.76 (gen 4) → 0.75 (gen 9) → within
±0.2 of zero for gens 10–19; the share of adversaries scoring exactly 0.0
rises from 0 % (gens 1–7) to 38 % (gen 18), and those become the elites
because 0 outranks every negative score. Heritability is nil: an elite's
fitness at gen g has Spearman −0.02 with its own fitness at g+1, and the
elite mean beats the offspring mean in 47 % of generations. The same collapse
appears in every version regardless of variation rate or fresh blood (env
max, last-5 mean: V1 0.61, V2 0.42, V3 0.21). This is why slides 11 and 15
could report "not cured by any knob": no variation knob can move a ceiling.

V4's `--env-skip-no-trade-matches` removes the adversary's only non-negative
outcomes, so late in a run selection turns *against* bear windows. That is
the most plausible reason the served bear share fell to 12.7 %, below the
21.7 % uniform rate, rather than merely decaying to it.

**Fix (as an added arm, not a replacement).** `EngineConfig.env_fitness =
"regret"`: the adversary earns, per served segment, max(0, buy-and-hold,
MA-crossover) − trader Sharpe, i.e. "where does the trader do worse than a
trivial policy". Bear regret goes to 0 once the trader learns cash-in-bear,
and the adversary is pushed to wherever the trader is currently wrong. V5
runs this as `coevolution_regret` beside the unchanged `coevolution` arm.

### F6. Selection is a lottery on both sides
In `population_seed47` (uniform windows, so no adversary effects) the mean
within-trader match-score sd is 1.17 against a between-trader fitness sd of
0.82 per generation: the noise of one trader's four matches exceeds the
spread PBT ranks on. Final-generation internal rank vs held-out Sharpe has
Spearman 0.02. This is the mechanism behind slide 12's "drift" and F5's nil
heritability, and a sharper statement than "parent pool of 2".

**Fix, cheap.** `final_traders/` now keeps every last-generation trader and
`compare_arms.py --final-traders` reports the per-seed population mean next
to the rank-0 pick. A shared per-generation exam (rank every trader on the
same segments) was proposed as a future variant at this audit date; it is
now implemented in `engine._shared_exam` and used by the final BTC node.

### F7. Smaller items
- Baselines paid no slippage while the agent paid 0.05 % per side
  (`baselines.py`). Both baselines now take `slippage`; default 0 keeps the
  V1–V4 numbers, `evaluate.py`/`compare_arms.py` pass the env's value.
- Slide 15 said test segments overlap and cited a block bootstrap; test
  segments are non-overlapping (stride 720) and the bootstrap is plain paired.
  Fixed in the .tex.
- "Test has no bull" depends on the trailing-label majority. By the
  segment's own net move the 9-segment window is 3 bear / 5 sideways / 1
  bull (the +15 % June 2026 window). `Segment.move_regime` is now reported
  alongside.
- `config.json` records the git revision. V1–V3 predate the untested-elite
  fix and will not reproduce bit-for-bit on current code.
- `run_walkforward.py` built its `EngineConfig` from 7 fields and V1-era
  defaults for the rest; it now shares `engine_cli.py` with
  `run_coevolution.py`, so a walk-forward fold's `config.json` differs from
  V5's only in mode and seed (verified).

## 2. Checked and found sound
Tournament selection index, fitness-sharing sign handling, env hall of fame
stores copies, PBT weight copy, scaler fit on train only, validation never
used for selection, uniform segment draw, test segments non-overlapping,
regime warm-up does not change any segment label, walk-forward folds re-fit
the scaler per fold, and the headline eval numbers reproduce from the frozen
models. Not audited: the feature-engineering notebook for lookahead (the
scripted version reproduces it exactly, so any such issue would be shared).

## 3. Runs launched at the audit date
The first V5 launch (2026-09-14 00:32) was killed 24 minutes in by a machine
shutdown, the same way V4 was lost. The engine now checkpoints every
generation and resumes (`--resume`), and the drivers skip finished runs, so
after any shutdown the whole plan is relaunched with one command:

    nohup bash ppo/run_all.sh >> ppo/runs/run_all.log 2>&1 &

(or, to make it automatic, the `@reboot` crontab line in `ppo/run_all.sh`).
Relaunched 2026-09-14 11:46 at commit c64db3b:

- `ppo/run_v5.sh` → `ppo/runs/v5/`: V4 config, 30 generations, 6 seeds, four
  arms (coevolution_regret, coevolution, population, single), ≈ 7 h, then
  `compare_arms.py` on `test.parquet` and `test_ext.parquet` with
  `--final-traders`.
- `ppo/run_walkforward.sh` → `ppo/runs/walkforward/`, runs after V5: V5
  engine flags, 20 generations, 6 folds (test windows Mar 2022 → Apr 2026),
  3 seeds for the population arms and 6 for single, ≈ 10 h, then aggregation
  and `compare_arms.py --walkforward-root`.

## 4. Decisions recorded at the audit date
1. Regret adversary runs as a fourth arm, not a replacement.
2. Walk-forward is run with V5's engine flags so the tables agree; whether it
   becomes the headline design is a write-up decision after the numbers land.
3. `compare_arms.py` reports both rank-0 and final-population mean; the
   write-up should quote the population mean as primary.
4. Per-regime tables carry both regime definitions.
5. Walk-forward uses 3 seeds for the population arms (≈ 10.5 h) rather than 6.
